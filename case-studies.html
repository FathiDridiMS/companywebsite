Telecommunications · Sovereign on-premises AI

On-premises AI platform for contract lifecycle management

ClientThe incumbent telecom operator in its market
FunctionLegal department
ScopeB2B, B2C and wholesale contract portfolios
DeploymentFully on-premises, local AI farm
Outcome

A sovereign AI platform positioned as an augmented legal assistant: it accelerates contract review, improves consistency across the legal function, surfaces risk earlier and makes the contract estate searchable in natural language, while contracts, models and inference all remain inside the operator's own infrastructure, and legal judgement stays with the lawyers.

View project details
Context

The operator manages a large and growing portfolio of contracts and amendments across consumer, business and wholesale perimeters, with a strong need for centralisation and standardisation. An enterprise document management platform was already in place, covering centralisation, security, traceability, classification, search and secure archival. In parallel, the operator was structuring a local AI farm to address sensitive scenarios such as assisted contract analysis while keeping processing and data in a controlled environment.

Challenge

Contract drafting, review and compliance checking were slow and inconsistent across a large legal function. AI could accelerate this work substantially, but contracts, regulatory documents and legal content are precisely the material that cannot be sent to public cloud AI services. Data sovereignty was a hard constraint, not a preference.

Solution
  • An AI layer built on top of the existing document repository rather than replacing it
  • Ingestion connectors reading contracts, amendments, internal reference material and regulatory corpora from the existing repository
  • OCR, normalisation, segmentation and metadata extraction pipeline feeding a vector database
  • Local LLM serving on the operator's own GPU infrastructure, orchestrated under Kubernetes or OpenShift
  • RAG orchestration providing contextual retrieval with source citation
  • A contractual web portal for drafting, validation workflow, versioning and dashboards, consuming the AI farm through secured APIs behind firewall/WAF and load balancer
  • AI capabilities: assisted drafting from dynamic templates, semantic analysis and risk clause detection, compliance checking, version comparison, obligation and deadline extraction, intelligent question-answering and assisted regulatory monitoring
  • Governance by design: human-in-the-loop validation, full traceability, explainability through cited extracts, role separation across lawyers, validators, administrators and auditors, and a hybrid AI-plus-business-rules approach
  • Security architecture: network isolation, encryption, RBAC, complete interaction logging, local retention of data and models
  • A four-phase roadmap: scoping, document foundation, priority AI use cases, then regulatory monitoring and risk mapping
Technologies
On-premises LLM servingDeepSeek / Llama / MistralRAG orchestrationVector databaseOCR pipelineKubernetes & OpenShiftGPU infrastructureSecured APIs

ClientThe incumbent telecom operator in its market
ScaleSeveral million fixed and mobile subscribers
PortalsConsumer and business self-care

Outcome

Consumer and business customers manage their lines, plans, invoices and payments in two secure self-care portals connected to the operator's core systems. The platforms strengthen the operator's digital presence, support new digital revenues and lay the foundation for loyalty programmes, advanced analytics and AI-driven personalisation.

View project details
Context

The operator serves several million fixed and mobile subscribers and plays a central role in national internet access. Its digital channels are the primary self-service route for consumers as well as businesses, ministries and institutions.

Challenge

Consumers faced fragmented journeys and could not manage several lines from a single account. Businesses had limited multi-user access, poor visibility over balances and invoices, and manually handled support that slowed resolution. Both portals had to meet the availability and security standards of a national operator.

Solution
  • Consumer portal: account creation by email or mobile number, up to 10 lines per account, profile management and automated welcome bonuses on activation
  • Full digital catalogue: voice top-ups (TT Cash, e-vouchers, recharge codes), internet plans, bespoke voice and data combinations, SMS and MMS packs and gifts

  • Digital billing: consolidated invoices by line or customer code, one-click payment of several invoices, e-invoice alerts by SMS and email, secure download
  • Business portal: multi-user accounts with role-based access, for hybrid, postpaid, data, cloud and VoIP accounts
  • Fleet and usage management for all company lines, data plans, eSIM activation and roaming control, with invoice export to PDF and Excel
  • 360° dashboard of active lines, balances and invoices, with integrated management of complaints, requests and tickets
  • Integration with Keycloak, Ericsson BSCS, billing systems, the on-premises SMS gateway, Microsoft Exchange and MPGS and Click to Pay payment gateways

Telecommunications · Digital commerce

E-shop rebuild on a headless e-commerce platform

ClientThe incumbent telecom operator in its market
ChannelsE-shop, B2B and B2C, one single account
PlatformGrandNode on Red Hat OpenShift
Team6 specialists, from architect to front end
Outcome

A single e-commerce platform where customers buy and manage their telecom services without journey breaks, with one login for e-shop, B2B and B2C, a shared basket and harmonised offers. The open, stateless architecture scales horizontally and prepares for cross-selling, journey personalisation and new digital services.

View project details
Context

The operator wanted to rebuild its online shop so consumers and businesses could buy devices, plans and services in the same experience as their self-care portals, on infrastructure it controls.

Challenge

Catalogues and pricing differed between the e-shop and the B2B and B2C channels, customers needed separate accounts, and the existing shop struggled with traffic peaks. The new platform had to integrate with existing backend APIs and run on the operator's container platform, with no licence dependency.

Solution
  • Headless e-commerce architecture with GrandNode (ASP.NET Core) for the backend and an Angular front end
  • Single Keycloak sign-on shared across e-shop, B2B and B2C
  • Dedicated microservices hosted in a dedicated OpenShift project and exposed via the external WSO2 gateway; backend APIs consumed via the internal WSO2 gateway
  • MongoDB database deployed on OpenShift, with one pod per component and sizing defined per environment
  • Dev, UAT and production environments for continuous integration and functional and performance testing
  • Harmonised product catalogue, centralised basket and checkout, with multi-store, B2B and B2C support
